# Why I'm Not Worried About Running Out of Work in the Age of AI

Dave Kellogg applies Jevons Paradox to knowledge work: when technology makes something more efficient, we don't do less of it — we do more. Demand expands to fill the new capacity.

---

> "There is always, always more work yet to do."

His proof point is business intelligence. After decades of advancement in BI tooling, organizations still lack sufficient data-informed decision-making. The tools got dramatically better; the work didn't shrink — it expanded into new applications and new questions that weren't even askable before.

---

Kellogg frames management itself as evidence of abundance: management fundamentally concerns prioritization and focus _because_ the space of possible work is unlimited. If work were scarce, you wouldn't need prioritization frameworks — you'd just do everything. The fact that every team needs to say "no" to most things proves the constraint was never volume of output.

---

On displacement: he draws the historical parallel to elevator operators and switchboard operators (society doesn't mourn the roles), and the auto industry decline (the transition was rough for any given autoworker, even if collectively beneficial). But knowledge workers have an advantage — transferable skills like writing, analysis, and design make sideways moves into adjacent roles more natural than manufacturing-to-engineering leaps.

---

His advice for knowledge workers: master AI tools aggressively, understand business outcomes not just tool mechanics, and "skate to where the puck is going" — toward the roles that manage, direct, and make judgment calls about AI-generated output.
