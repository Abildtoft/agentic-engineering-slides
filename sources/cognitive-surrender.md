# Cognitive Surrender

Wharton's latest AI study points to a hard truth: the "AI writes, humans review" model is breaking down.

Steven D. Doshi and Gideon Neel (Wharton School, University of Pennsylvania) introduce the concept of **Cognitive Surrender** — when you stop verifying what the AI tells you, and you don't even realize you stopped. It's different from offloading (like using a calculator). With offloading, you know the tool did the work. With surrender, your brain recodes the AI's answer as YOUR judgment. You genuinely believe you thought it through yourself.

They frame AI as a **System 3** — extending Kahneman's System 1 (fast intuition) and System 2 (slow analysis) with an external cognitive system that operates outside your brain. When you use it enough, cognitive surrender kicks in.

## Key data

Across 3 preregistered studies with 1,372 participants and 9,593 trials:

- People turned to AI on over 50% of questions
- When AI was correct, people followed it 92.7% of the time
- When AI was wrong, they still followed it 79.8% of the time
- Without AI, baseline accuracy was 45.8%
- With correct AI, accuracy jumped to 71.0%
- With incorrect AI, accuracy dropped to 31.5% — worse than having no AI
- AI boosted confidence by 11.7 percentage points, even when the answers were wrong

Time pressure did not eliminate the effect. Incentives and feedback reduced it but did not remove it. The people most resistant tended to score higher on fluid intelligence and need for cognition — making this a cognitive architecture problem, not a laziness problem.

## Paper

Steven D. Doshi & Gideon Neel, "Thinking — Fast, Slow, and Artificial: How AI is Reshaping Human Reasoning and the Rise of Cognitive Surrender" (Wharton School, University of Pennsylvania)
