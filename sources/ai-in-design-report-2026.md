# AI in Design Report 2026

Designer Fund and Foundation Capital (two investment firms), second annual edition. Blog summary by Robyn Park headed 20 May 2026 (page metadata shows later edits in October). Survey of more than 900 designers at startups, enterprises and agencies in 60+ countries, plus 25+ interviews and case studies (Sierra, Linear, Shopify, Stripe, Anthropic).

## Findings

- **Usage.** "Weekly AI usage for design tasks has jumped from 54% to 91%, and 75% use it daily." The average designer regularly uses 7 off-the-shelf AI tools, up from 3.
- **Shipping code.** "Half of respondents have pushed AI-generated code to production" — product and brand designers included, not just design engineers.
- **Roles.** "65% of designers said they're taking on more product or engineering responsibilities," and "40% said the reverse is also happening."
- **Collaboration.** "Only 5% of respondents said collaboration had decreased because of AI" a year earlier; "this year, that number rose 4x to 20%." "Work is moving faster, but some of the connective tissue of collaboration hasn't kept pace."
- **Expectations against policy.** "73% of designers say they feel rising expectations around output, quality, and speed," but "only 28% of leaders say their companies have made formal updates to evaluation, comp, or hiring." "Just 8% have changed performance metrics, and only 4% have adjusted compensation structure."
- **Learning.** "Peer learning more than tripled year over year (from 24% to 80%)," while "taking recommendations from leadership dropped from 32% to 16%." "Designers at companies with a culture of tinkering are 2x more likely to say they feel more creative and capable, even though they also face a higher bar."
- **Hiring.** 50% of leaders put more emphasis on AI fluency when hiring; only 5% put less emphasis on execution quality.

- **Section 2, "The Crossover Runs Both Ways."** Half of designers have shipped AI-generated code to production: the third stat on the slide. Pairs with [[figma-2026-ai-report]].
- **Section 4, "Single-Player Mode."** The 5% → 20% collaboration figure is the slide's evidence line, alongside [[jenny-wanger-building-alone-faster]].
- **Section 6.** The 73% / 28% / 8% / 4% gap supports "This is not a tooling upgrade. It's a different operating model." on What Survives. Peer learning at 80% and the tinkering-culture finding support Where to Start: the organisation learns from finished pilots shared between peers, not from a strategy memo.
- **Bias.** Investors in design-led companies, and a self-selected sample of designers. A sample that likes AI makes the rise in "AI reduced collaboration" more striking, not less.
