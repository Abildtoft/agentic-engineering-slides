# Agentic Autonomy Levels

Addy Osmani, published 2 July 2026.

Osmani separates autonomy into two dimensions: how far one agent can act without intervention, and how much multi-agent coordination the system can perform. He then maps them into six levels: assist, supervised action, scoped task delegation, goal-driven autonomy, parallel delegation, and managed-by-exception orchestration.

The correct level follows risk, reversibility, and available verification—not the task label or a desire to appear AI-native. Every autonomous run needs a contract covering goal, scope, non-goals, tools and permissions, stopping condition, independent evidence, escalation, and budget. Higher levels introduce specific failure modes: drift, stale assumptions, merge conflicts, silent spend, review queues, and alert fatigue.

Osmani's practical test for autonomy asks how quickly the system will detect error, how cleanly it can undo the action, and what independent evidence would establish success. Trust grows by accumulating accepted runs with defensible evidence.
