# Cognitive Surrender

Addy Osmani, published 5 May 2026.

Osmani applies a Wharton concept to software engineering. Cognitive offloading delegates the mechanics while preserving independent judgment; cognitive surrender occurs when the model's answer becomes the user's answer before the user forms a view of their own.

The danger is borrowed confidence: plausible output, passing checks, and a confident review summary can create endorsement without comprehension. In engineering this appears when people approve code they cannot explain, accept a model's review instead of reading evidence, or defend design choices whose reasoning they never constructed.

The protective pattern is to preserve an independent comparison point: state expectations before asking, inspect evidence rather than summaries, separate maker and reviewer roles, and ensure a human remains accountable for the merge.
