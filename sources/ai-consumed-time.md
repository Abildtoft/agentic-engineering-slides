# AI Was Supposed to Free My Time. It Consumed It.

Katie Parrott, Every (Working Overtime column), March 9, 2026.

The core thesis: AI doesn't reduce workload — it intensifies work through psychological mechanisms that make stopping difficult. The pattern resembles gambling.

## Three mechanisms of intensification

UC Berkeley researchers Aruna Ranganathan and Xingqi Maggie Ye observed ~200 employees at a tech company over eight months and identified three ways AI intensifies work:

1. **Task expansion** — people absorb responsibilities they previously outsourced. Parrott's own role expanded to include "building AI-powered editorial workflows" on top of her writing duties.
2. **Blurred boundaries** — prompting "felt closer to chatting than to formal labor." Workers send a "quick last prompt" before leaving their desk, letting the AI work while they step away. Work bleeds into personal time.
3. **Multitasking** — managing multiple AI threads creates constant context-switching and "a growing pile of open tasks," leaving minds feeling "like a browser with 40 tabs open."

## The slot machine

Drawing on B.F. Skinner's 1950s research, variable-ratio reinforcement schedules (unpredictable rewards) create the most powerful, hardest-to-break habits. AI researcher M. Karen Shen (UBC) calls this the **"AI genie phenomenon"** — the sense that one more wish might solve persistent problems.

Within this, Shen identifies **"epistemic rabbit holes"**: cycles where "each AI response partially satisfies but opens a new question." Never quite enough to stop, always enough to continue.

Parrott's personal anecdote: she began configuring her AI assistant "Margot" on a Friday afternoon intending to spend "a few hours, tops." Twelve hours later she had written essays, rebuilt her website, and added app features. She went to bed around 1 a.m. but soon "launched herself out of bed, rushing to her desk, and typing in all caps."

## FOBO: Fear of Becoming Obsolete

Ernst & Young 2025 survey:
- **54%** of employees feel they're "falling behind their peers in AI use"
- **85%** are learning on their own time
- **83%** report self-taught knowledge

The fear loop: fear sends you to the laptop → tinkering produces wins → wins feel good → success expands perceived obligations → repeat. "The fear keeps me sitting at the machine. The slot machine keeps me pulling the lever."

## On solutions

The article suggests intentional pauses, batched notifications, protected focus windows, time for social connection, and analog activities with natural endpoints. Organizations may not create structural safeguards because "workers voluntarily take on more and produce faster without being asked."
