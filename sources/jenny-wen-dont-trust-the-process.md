# Don't "Trust the Process"

Jenny Wen (Design Lead at Anthropic, formerly Director of Design at Figma), keynote at Hatch Conference, Berlin, September 2025. Known through Simon Willison's link post of 24 January 2026, which quotes the talk's hypothesis.

## The argument

- **The process was built for expensive building.** The traditional sequence of user research, then personas, user journeys and wireframes, all before anything is built, assumed that building was the slow and costly step. With AI, it may no longer fit.
- **Lean on prototypes instead.** Prototypes are now fast and accessible enough to be the way you explore, not the thing you make after exploring.
- **The hypothesis, verbatim:** "In a world where anyone can make anything — what matters is your ability to choose and curate what you make."

Willison's addition: AI-assisted programming lowers the cost of building the wrong thing, so teams can take more risks and explore a problem space more thoroughly. He notes that he is a compulsive prototyper, so the argument suits his existing biases.

## Deck fit

- **Section 5, "Code is work an AI can check. Good design still isn't."** Wen's answer to the scene she describes in Section 2 ([[jenny-wen-design-in-ai-era]]): when engineers ship a working version before a designer finishes exploring, the scarce skill is choosing between versions, not producing them. This is her own wording, where the Section 2 slide carries Lenny Rachitsky's summary.
- **Same claim, second voice.** Paul Graham's "When anyone can make anything, the big differentiator is what you choose to make" ([[taste-even-more-important]]) came on 14 February 2026, about five months after the keynote.
- **Pairs with "The prototype is the new brief"** ([[prototype-is-the-new-prd]]): prototypes make options cheap, which is exactly what moves the work to choosing.
- **Careful with "taste as a moat".** On Lenny's Podcast, Wen says AI will likely get better at taste and judgment, and that designers may be holding onto taste too tightly; what survives is that someone has to be accountable for what ships. The deck's slide therefore ends on owning the choice, not on taste.
