# The Agency Ladder

Addy Osmani: "High agency is the art of knowing when to delegate, when to inspect, when to stop, and when to own the result of a process."

Seven rungs, least to most agency:

1. **Flag** — "There's a problem." Then walks away; it becomes someone else's problem.
2. **Execute** — "Hand me the fix, I'll ship it."
3. **Diagnose** — "Here's the problem, and the cause."
4. **Propose** — "...and a few ways to fix it."
5. **Recommend** — "...and here's the one I'd pick." Good engineers live here from day one.
6. **Resolve** — "Found it. Fixed it. Looping you in." You earn your way up to this.
7. **Discern** — "Found it. Not worth fixing. Moving on." The rare apex — knowing what NOT to do.

The AI angle: agents will run rungs 1 through 6 for you, and all of it is getting cheap fast. What doesn't get cheap: deciding it wasn't worth doing, deciding the evidence is good enough to ship, being the person who can explain why when it turns out you were wrong.

"Agency was never about how much you can do. It's about how much of the problem you still own after it leaves your hands."
