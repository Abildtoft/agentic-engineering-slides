# Act With Agency

Mitchell Hashimoto (HashiCorp, Ghostty), X post, 11 September 2026.

Hashimoto argues that an idea matters far less than the agency of the people executing it. Across the most successful people he has known and worked with, the difference between mediocre and excellent work is "predominantly one of agency": they don't wait for things to happen to them, for someone else to act, to teach them, or to hand them the path — they go and find a way.

The underlying belief he names as the superpower: the world around you is completely mutable. "Look around the room you're sitting in. Look at everything. Every noun. It almost all exists because a person willed it into existence. Nothing is stopping you from doing the same."

He also lands a sharp corollary on idea-ownership: dismissing someone else's success with "I had that idea first" is a self-own — if the idea was the same, the difference was you.

Closing line: "Number one tip: act with agency."

## Deck fit

- A second, human-side sense of *agency* alongside Osmani's agency ladder (sources/agency-ladder.md): the ladder is about how much of the problem you still own; Hashimoto is about whether you move at all.
