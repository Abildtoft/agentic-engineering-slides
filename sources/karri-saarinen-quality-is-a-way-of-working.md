# Quality Is a Way of Working, Not a Cleanup Sprint

Karri Saarinen (co-founder and CEO of Linear), X post, 5 October 2026. Captured from a screenshot shared by the speaker. The post in full:

> Frantically fixing a confusing product caused by frantic shipping is just more of the same.
>
> Pause and step back. Get the team clear on what you’re building and why. Have clarity to guide the shape, what ships and what doesn’t.
>
> Quality is a way of working, not a cleanup sprint.

He doesn't mention AI. The deck supplies that context: agents make frantic shipping cheaper than it has ever been.

## Linear's own practice

Tuomas Artman, Saarinen's co-founder, "Quality Wednesdays: How we trained our team to see what doesn't work" (linear.app/now/quality-wednesdays, 13 August 2025). After a 2023 offsite, the engineering team gave its Wednesday team meeting, one of three standups a week, to small polish fixes. "Many of these quality fixes take 30 minutes or less", and "None should take more than an hour or so." "Over the last two years we’ve completed more than 1,000 small acts of polish", tracked in Linear with a "quality" label.

The reasoning is the last line of Saarinen's post, a year before he wrote it: "We needed quality to be a habit, like brushing your teeth. Something you just do without thinking too much about it. No one ever says they don’t have time to brush their teeth." And: "attention to quality only works when it’s a habit".

Worth having because it shows the line is not a slogan. The company runs it on a weekly clock.

## Deck fit

- **Section 5, the closing slide.** It answers the accumulation slide ([[on-subtraction]], [[thdxr-llm-challenges]]). That slide says products die from accumulation; this one says what not to do once they have. thdxr's "100x more value in fixing what we have and improving our process of how we build things" has two halves, and Saarinen says the first alone, done frantically, is more of the same. "On Subtraction" makes the same move: "think slower, then act fast. slow down after the burst."
- **The section tagline in a practitioner's words.** Clear on what and why is choosing the work; clarity about what ships and what doesn't is setting the bar; quality as a way of working is improving the loop.
- **Section 2 callback.** The deck already quotes Saarinen on the middle of software work ([[disappearing-middle-of-software-work]]). This is what happens when that middle compresses and the team still decides the old way. [[linear-next]] is the same company's product thesis.
- **Tension with Section 6.** "Pause and step back" can sound like the defensive posture the Section 6 slide warns against ([[alfred-lin-play-offense]]): "Wait for clarity. It feels safe. It isn't." It is not the same thing: Lin's defender waits for the environment to settle, while Saarinen asks the team to produce clarity itself. It is the same distinction as "the fastest way to build is to find the cheapest way to learn".
- **Section 4, notes only.** Single-Player Mode's "Collaboration you put off doesn't disappear. It comes back as design and technical debt." and Willison's "Having poor quality code from an agent is a choice that you make." make the same claim from the debt side. "Get the team clear" is the team-scale answer to single-player mode.
