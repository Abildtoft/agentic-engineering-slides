# The Orchestration Tax

Addy Osmani, published 24 May 2026.

Starting agents is cheap; integrating their work is not. Osmani treats the human reviewer as the serial component in a concurrent system: every architectural judgment, merge decision, and mental-model reload routes through one limited processor. Adding producers beyond that review rate deepens the queue without increasing shipped throughput.

The tax appears as context switching, ambient vigilance, shallow reviews, and cognitive surrender. His operating advice is to scale the fleet to review capacity, separate bounded background tasks from judgment-heavy work, batch reviews, make agents prove deterministic conditions themselves, and protect the human's best attention for decisions machines cannot settle.

Feeling busy is therefore a misleading activity metric. The relevant measure is accepted, understood work reaching the main branch; a dashboard full of agents can coexist with very little trustworthy output.
