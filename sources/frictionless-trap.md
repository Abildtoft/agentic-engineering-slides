# The Frictionless Trap

Søren Christensen argues that AI's real risk is not output quality, but the loss of productive struggle that makes work feel authored, understood, and personally meaningful.

https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/frictionless-trap-s%C3%B8ren-christensen-n1cce/

## Core argument

AI can turn professionals from thinkers into validators: the work still gets done, often impressively, but the human is no longer "inside the thinking." The cognitive struggle that used to build ownership, judgment, and competence gets outsourced.

Christensen frames friction as constructive rather than merely inefficient. Drawing on ideas from anthropology, psychology, and craft, he argues that resistance is how people form meaning, learn, and develop durable relationships with their work.

The article's strongest distinction is between two ways of using AI:

- **Frictionless AI** generates the thought so the human can approve it.
- **Friction-producing AI** challenges the human's thought, pushes back on weak reasoning, and makes the person go deeper.

The second mode is slower, but it preserves the cognitive struggle that makes the output feel owned.

## Useful deck angle

This reinforces the cognitive debt section:

- Cognitive debt is not only about losing a system mental model.
- It is also about losing the struggle through which the mental model becomes yours.
- The goal is not to use AI less, but to design workflows where AI creates productive friction instead of removing all friction.

## Presenter phrasing

"The trap is not AI. The trap is the frictionless path: letting the model do the cognitive heavy lifting, then calling our approval process productivity."
