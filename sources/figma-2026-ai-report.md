# Figma's 2026 AI Report: Can AI Help Us Collaborate Better?

Madeline Stafford (content strategist) and Shane Johnston (researcher), Figma blog, 24 June 2026, with a full report on figma.com/reports. Three years of data: 8,403 survey responses and 639 qualitative interviews with designers, developers and product managers, across ten markets (the US, UK, Japan, Germany, France, Canada and Australia, plus Brazil, India and South Korea this year).

## Findings

- **Crossover, both directions, 2025 to 2026.** "In the last year, the number of designers participating in development doubled to 41%, and the number of developers doing design work rose from 44% to 60%." The chart gives the designer figure as 21% → 41%.
- **Collaboration.** 41% say AI meaningfully changes how teams work together, "compared to only 7% two years ago." Framing: "AI used to be a solo act" and "Teams are turning their single-player workflows into multiplayer ones on the canvas."
- **The canvas.** 76% do at least half their work on the canvas, six in ten spend the bulk of their time there, and one in five developers would rather start projects there.
- **Design's importance.** 90% say design is at least as important as before AI; nearly six in ten say it is more important; 65% of developers say the same.
- **Adoption patterns** (from the chart): Unified 36%, Directive 27%, Grassroots 20%, Nascent 18%.

## Deck fit

- **Section 2, "The Crossover Runs Both Ways."** The two crossover figures are the measured version of [[rise-of-the-product-engineer]] and the RolesBlur diagram's convergence claim.
- **Section 4, "Single-Player Mode" (presenter notes).** The counterpoint: Figma's respondents say AI is making teams *more* collaborative, while [[ai-in-design-report-2026]] finds the share saying it reduced collaboration quadrupled. Both can hold; the direction depends on the team.
- **Bias.** Figma sells the canvas this report favours, and the data is self-reported. Use it as a direction, the way the deck treats company-reported adoption figures in Section 1.
